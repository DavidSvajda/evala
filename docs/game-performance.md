# Rating-relative game performance · model v3

The displayed performance is Evala's experimental rating-relative heuristic. It has no independently measured prediction error and is not an implementation of the proprietary Chess.com Game Rating. It is separate from the Lichess-based whole-game accuracy percentage. A configured Maia level is not a human-rating calibration label.

The former linear model multiplied the quality residual by 24 and then by n/(n+15), flattening strong short-game results. Its depth>=12 selection also changed the sample between engine presets, especially in complex positions. The revised implementation retains post-opening, non-forced, non-repeated, contested decisions with depth>=8. It requires at least twelve such decisions and >=90% eligible coverage. Depth 8–11 and Lite evidence are explicitly provisional. Missing evaluations fail closed. Clearly decisive positions and forced moves do not inflate the sample.

Decision quality is recomputed from the stored before/after evaluations. The mean of arithmetic and harmonic decision accuracies reduces dilution of serious errors by routine accurate moves. The initial rating comes from WhiteElo/BlackElo. The existing heuristic expected-quality prior E=clamp(55+0.015*rating,60,95) is retained and is not an empirical calibration. The new relative mapping is rating + 400/ln(10) * (logit(Q/100)-logit(E/100)), bounded to 100–3500 and rounded to fifty. Q is capped to 0.5–99.5 to keep extremes finite. Accuracy here is a quality surrogate, not a win probability, so the transformation is a display heuristic rather than a statistically justified Elo estimate. Future calibration must validate the prior and mapping with Evala's exact search and scoring protocol, separated player-level train/holdout samples and platform/time-control strata.

Small samples, shallow evidence, Lite and large opponent-rating gaps widen the displayed allowance. The range is not a statistical confidence interval. Point estimates are no longer multiplied by sample reliability, and changing only metadata depth does not change the inferred point when evaluations are identical. No engine reanalysis is required to update saved games because estimates are derived at render time.

Bounded real-game check: completed public game https://www.chess.com/game/live/186535260616, recorded ratings 1697/1714, 52 plies. The public PGN is stripped of clocks/comments in the regression fixture. Stockfish 19 Full NNUE single-thread fast search at 300ms/depth14/MultiPV3 gave Lichess-based accuracy about 85.4/98.1. The former model returned ~1750 for White and insufficient evidence for Black (8 eligible decisions); v3 includes 19/16 decisions and returns ~1800/~2100 provisionally. A separate 500ms/depth20 bounded review gave ~1750/~2000. The user's Chess.com screenshot shows 1800/2350; that is comparison evidence, not ground truth or a fitting target. No numerical agreement or population-level calibration is claimed. These two runs do not benchmark the user's laptop or validate every position/preset.
